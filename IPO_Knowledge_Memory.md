### Position-sizing rules at ₹1 lakh
- Max **1 lot per IPO**. No second lot, no family accounts to "improve odds" beyond one's own.
- Max **3 concurrent blocked applications** (~₹45,000). Money is frozen T+5; congestion weeks can trap all cash.
- Max **2 open listing-trade positions** at once.
- Long-term core positions sourced from this pool: ≤ **40% (₹40,000)** total, ≤ 1 lot per name.
- Keep ≥ **25% (₹25,000) dry** at all times for a genuinely superior issue (e.g., Jio).
- Portfolio circuit-breaker: if cumulative realised loss in a rolling month hits **₹10,000 (10%)**, stop applying, re-read this file, review process before the next application.
- Never average down without a fresh full fundamental review.

## 3. DECISION RULES

### 3.1 Hard filters (automatic SKIP for both objectives)
1. SME platform IPO (lot ≥ ₹2 lakh; manipulation and liquidity risk).
2. Final (Day-3 close) total subscription < 3x or QIB < 2x.
3. Critical governance flag: promoter fraud/SEBI action, auditor resignation, undisclosed litigation, pledging.
4. Mainboard "micro-issue" ≤ ₹200 Cr run by a Tier-3 BRLM (see 7.2) — FY26 evidence: 8 of 10 such issues are −20% to −78% within a year.
5. Applying because of GMP alone, brand name alone, or "everyone is applying".

### 3.2 Listing-gain APPLY conditions (all must hold at Day-3 2:30 PM)
- QIB (ex-anchor) ≥ **5x** (≥ 20–25x for issues priced > 50x P/E).
- Total ≥ **15x**.
- Anchor book contains ≥ 3 recognised domestic MFs/insurers.
- BRLM Tier 1 or Tier 2 (Section 7.2).
- Sector not in a confirmed down-cycle (Section 7.1).
- GMP present but **< 30%** (GMP above ~30% has systematically over-promised).
- Retail subscription is a cost, not a signal: allotment odds ≈ 1 / retail-x.

### 3.3 Long-term BUY-THROUGH-IPO conditions (rare — expect 1–3 per year)
- Profitable, positive FCF in ≥ 2 of last 3 years, ROCE ≥ 15%, D/E ≤ 1.
- Fresh issue ≥ 50% of proceeds going to growth or deleveraging, promoters retain ≥ 50%.
- P/E ≤ 1.3× RHP peer median (adjusted for growth/ROCE), or dominant franchise where full price is defensible (exchange, depository, monopoly utility).
- Default for good-but-full-priced companies: **WAIT FOR LISTING AND PRICE DISCOVERY**. Add-zone = ~25x trailing or after 2 clean quarters.

### 3.4 Scoring weights (unchanged from framework)
Business 15 · Financial quality 15 · Growth 10 · Governance 15 · Valuation 20 · Structure 10 · Industry 5 · Demand 5 · Downside 5. Interpretation: ≥80 strong · 70–79 apply · 60–69 borderline · 50–59 usually avoid · <50 avoid. **The score governs long-term verdicts. Listing-gain verdicts use 3.2 and may diverge.**

## 3.5 STANDARD IPO SNAPSHOT (required at the top of every IPO evaluation)
A 12-line chain, one line each, before the long-form analysis. Write "n/a" where a step does not apply to the business model (e.g., order book for a retailer, capacity for an exchange).

1. **Industry** — structure, growth, cyclicality, regulation, where we are in the cycle
2. **Business economics** — what is sold, to whom, moat, pricing power
3. **Unit economics** — margin per unit/order/customer, take rate, gross-to-EBITDA bridge
4. **Capacity** — installed vs utilised, expansion funded by the issue, time to fill
5. **Order book** — size vs revenue, execution period, concentration
6. **Financial statements** — 3-yr revenue/EBITDA/PAT trend, margin direction, one-offs
7. **Cash conversion** — CFO/EBITDA, FCF sign, working-capital days trend, payables stretch
8. **Capital allocation** — use of proceeds, debt paydown vs growth, past capex returns (ROCE trend)
9. **Management quality** — promoter record, holding post-issue, OFS size, governance flags, BRLM tier
10. **Valuation & peers** — P/E / EV-EBITDA / P-B at upper band vs RHP peer median, adjusted for growth and ROCE
11. **Downside scenario** — what the stock is worth if the bear case lands; % below issue price
12. **Thesis-breakers** — 3–5 observable events that would end the trade or the hold

Then the two verdicts (listing / long-term), the Day-3 QIB threshold for this issue, and position size.

## 3.6 RULEBOOK v1.3 (5 Oct 2026) — supersedes conflicting lines elsewhere in this file
1. **Listing-trade apply rule:** Mainboard, final QIB (ex-anchor) ≥ 25x AND total ≥ 15x on the post-3 PM Day-3 print. Final QIB < 5x → skip (0 gains in every case so far: A-One +3%, Hero −2.4%, Manipal −2.7%, ARCIL 0%).
2. **Fundamental flags veto holds, not listing trades.** Pre-IPO profit spikes, rich P/E and heavy OFS did not stop strong listings once QIB ≥ ~75x (Moneyview spike → +58%; SS Retail 79x P/E → +47–51%). They still decide the long-term verdict.
3. **Only a post-3 PM exchange/Chittorgarh Day-3 print is valid.** Midday third-party data nearly caused a wrong Moneyview skip (QIB 3.8x midday → 219x at 3:39 PM). No fresh print → ask for a screenshot.
4. **Micro-issues (< ₹250 Cr):** no auto-skip with a Tier-1/2 BRLM — run the Day-3 check. Still skip if Tier-3 BRLM or retail heading past ~75x (EV < ~₹100 per application). Adroit (+76%) was skipped without a Day-3 check — process miss; ArMee (−27%) skip was right.
5. **GMP = direction only.** It under-predicted QIB > 150x books (SS Retail 32% GMP → +50%; Moneyview 24–41% → +58%) and over-predicted retail/NII-led books (Kanohar, Glass Wall, Skyways). The old "GMP > 30% over-promises" rule is retired.
6. **Allotment is a lottery; volume of qualifying applications is the edge.** EV per qualifying application ≈ ₹200–500. Apply to every issue that clears rule 1 (within regime limits) and keep ≥ 2 lots of free cash on closing days.
7. **Unchanged:** one lot at cut-off; SME excluded; sell at open or split when open is strong; mega-IPOs are holds, not trades; fundamentals alone decide long-term verdicts.

## 3.7 MACRO REGIME CHECK (run on every "macro check" and before every IPO decision)
Track: US 10-yr Treasury yield, Brent crude, India gold rate (and spot $/oz), USD/INR, India VIX.

| Regime | Signals | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Stress** | US 10-yr > 5.5% AND Brent > $110 | No IPO listing trades; only staged buys of quality long-term names |
| **Caution** (set late Sep 2026: 10-yr ~5.28%, Brent ~$102, VIX ~13.7, heavy FII selling) | Between the two | Max 1 open listing trade; sell at open; no Day-2 holds |
| **Normal** | US 10-yr < 5.0% AND Brent < $90 | Standard rules; IT tranches proceed as planned |
| **Fear signal** | Gold rising while US yields stay high | Trigger a check-in |

Transmission to India: higher US yields → FII outflows, weaker rupee; higher crude → inflation, weaker rupee, no RBI cuts. A US–Iran deal on Hormuz is the biggest single swing factor for crude.
VWRA: keep SIP; lump sum only if global stocks fall 10–15% from highs. Optional: shift ₹4,000/month from VWRA to Nifty 50 while Nifty P/E < ~20; revert when > ~21.

## 4. TWO PROBLEMS, TWO TOOLKITS

| | Listing-gain trade | Long-term hold |
|---|---|---|
| What predicts success | Final QIB demand, anchor quality, BRLM tier, sector momentum, float size | Business quality, cash flow, sector cycle, entry valuation, promoter quality |
| What does NOT predict | Starting P/E (within reason), OFS%, Day-2 subscription | Listing-day pop, GMP, subscription multiple |
| Hit rate observed | ~80% positive when 3.2 conditions met (Aug–Sep 2026, n=16) | ~53% positive at 6–15 months for all FY26 Mainboard IPOs bought at issue (n≈108) |
| Correct default | Apply if thresholds met; sell into strength | Wait for price discovery; buy later on weakness or after results |

**Core insight:** Listing gains are a demand-timing trade; long-term returns are a business-quality bet. The IPO price is set by the seller to be full for the long-term buyer but leave room for the day-one trader — that is why the two verdicts often disagree and both can be right.

## 5. SUBSCRIPTION MECHANICS THAT MATTER

- **QIBs bid in the last 3 hours of Day 3.** Day-2 QIB below 1x means nothing. In the 17-Sept-2026 cohort, 5 of 6 issues had Day-2 QIB < 1.2x; three finished 78–177x.
- **Allotment adverse selection:** heavily oversubscribed (good) IPOs give you a lottery ticket (retail 15–30x → 3–7% odds); undersubscribed (bad) IPOs give you the full lot. Expected value of one application in a hot issue is small (₹150–400). Therefore the framework's conservatism is cheap; the real risk is applying to the issue everyone else skipped.
- **NII/sNII at 50x+** is largely leverage-funded and can reverse; give it little weight. **bNII** tells you about HNI conviction more than sNII.
- **Retail-led books with QIB ≈ 1x** (e.g., Jindal Supreme Day 1: retail 11.6x, QIB 1.0x) are the pattern that produced Skyways (−10%). Wait for QIB.
- **Anchor lock-in:** 50% released at 30 days, 50% at 90 days. First natural supply overhang for any hold.

**Rule:** Sector momentum boosts listing-day outcomes and destroys 12-month outcomes when it reverses. Use sector as a **listing-gain input**, and as a **reason for caution** on long-term holds.

### 7.2 Merchant banker (BRLM) tiers — empirical
- **Tier 1** (Kotak, Axis Capital, ICICI Sec, JM Financial, IIFL, Citi, JPM, Morgan Stanley, Goldman, Nomura, SBI Caps, Jefferies, HSBC, BofA): dominate the winners list. Still had losers (Oswal Pumps −56, Kalpataru −38, Brigade −38, Studds −31) — a Tier-1 name is necessary, not sufficient.
- **Tier 2** (Motilal Oswal, Nuvama, Equirus, DAM Capital, Avendus, Ambit, IDBI Caps, Anand Rathi, Choice, Monarch, Elara, Beeline, PL Capital): mixed; judge case by case.
- **Tier 3** (Pantomath, Systematix, Interactive Financial, Unistone, Sarthi, Smart Horizon, GYR, Fedex, Arihant, Mefcom, Nirbhay): FY26 record: Pantomath — Highway −37, Vikran −42, Dev Acc −44, Glottis −55, Regaal −21 (Vidya Wires +63 the exception); Systematix — Indogulf −41, Mangal −52, Jaro −47; Interactive — Shree Ram −65, Anlon −78. **Treat Tier-3-led Mainboard issues like SME issues: default skip.**

### 7.3 Issue size
- **₹100–250 Cr Mainboard "micro" issues:** 8 of ~12 in FY26 are down 20–78%. Highest dispersion, negative skew, poor post-listing liquidity. Skip unless Tier-1/2 BRLM + clean fundamentals.
- **₹500–1,500 Cr:** the sweet spot for listing trades — enough institutional weight to hold the open, small enough float to lock circuits (Karamtara ₹875 Cr).
- **> ₹5,000 Cr:** modest listing pops (−8% to +13%), good 12-month record for quality franchises. Apply for the hold, not the pop.

### 7.4 Valuation at issue
- No clean relationship between starting P/E and 12-month return (Sedemac 87x +131%; Vikram 86x −51%). Loss-making names split too (Ather +388%, Meesho +92% vs JSW Cement −21%).
- What P/E does predict: **downside severity when things go wrong.** Every solar loser was priced 25–86x into a cyclical peak.
- Practical rule: valuation is a **margin-of-safety filter for holds**, not a predictor of listing behaviour. For listing trades, ignore P/E above the "would institutions still bid?" threshold — the QIB book answers that.

### 7.5 Grey Market Premium (GMP)
- Direction: right ~85% of the time. Magnitude: realised open ≈ 50–100% of GMP in normal cases; **≈ 20–30% of GMP when GMP > 30%** (Kanohar, Glass Wall) or when valuation is extreme (Symbiotec 19% → 0%).
- GMP of **₹0 on a brand name** (Hero Motors, 90x P/E) = market has already rejected the price. Respect it.
- Rule: plan on 50–70% of GMP; flag > 30% as a risk, not a bonus; never let GMP override a fundamentals skip.

### 7.6 OFS vs fresh issue
- Listing day: irrelevant. Rentomojo 88% OFS listed +19%; Symbiotec 91% OFS listed 0%.
- 12 months: relevant. OFS-heavy issues transfer ownership from informed sellers to you; fresh-issue growth capital is the better long-term structure. NSE (100% OFS) is the exception justified by franchise quality.

### 7.7 Behavioural / sentiment patterns
- Retail crowds chase brand names (Hero Motors retail 2.4x with QIB 0.01x) and high GMP micro-issues (Manika 35% GMP on 7% revenue growth). Both are the wrong side of adverse selection.
- After a big win, the urge is to redeploy immediately into the next open issue. **The best next action after a win is usually nothing.**
- Listing-day circuit boards (100% bid, zero offers) look unstoppable; day-two behaviour is a coin flip (Pranav faded from +31% to +10% in an hour; Kanohar rose from +6% to +19%).
- Everyone who couldn't sell at Day-1 upper circuit is a seller at Day-2 open.

## 8. LISTING-DAY EXECUTION PLAYBOOK

**Pre-decided rules (write these down at allotment):**
1. Open ≥ +12% → sell on listing day.
2. Open −5% to +12% → exit within first week; do not drift into a hold.
3. Open < −10% → don't panic-sell in minute one; re-evaluate within the week; −25% is the hard stop.

**Mechanics:**
- Pre-open (9:00–9:45): read discovered price and bid/offer ratio. 95%+ bid side = likely upward push at 10:00.
- Use **limit orders**, 1–2 rupees under the prevailing price; never market orders in the first 2 minutes.
- Listing-day band = ±10% around discovered price (issues > ₹250 Cr; Karamtara: 320 → 288–352). At upper circuit, a limit sell AT the circuit price fills instantly (buyers queued, zero offers).
- Day-2 band resets around Day-1 close.

**Default split when the open is strong (validated 17 Sept 2026):**
- Sell **~65–70%** at or near the open (or at upper circuit if hit) → recovers ~100% of capital when open ≥ +25%.
- Hold **~30–35%** with a hard stop at the discovered open price and a **time stop of 1–2 sessions**.
- Stops move up only. No re-entry after selling.
- Per-share asymmetry at UC: next-day cap gives +10% on the remainder; a fade to open costs −9%; a locked lower circuit (no bids) costs −19% and cannot be exited — this is why the sold tranche must already lock the profit.

## 9. LONG-TERM HOLDING RULES

- Buying a good company through an IPO at a full price is a **timing** error, not a **selection** error. In FY26, HDB, NSDL, Canara Robeco, Vikram Solar, Oswal Pumps all traded below issue within months — patience was rewarded.
- Entry strategy: half after 2–4 weeks of price discovery **inside the add-zone**; the rest after the first or second quarterly result.
- Watch dates: anchor unlock (30d / 90d); first quarterly results; lock-in expiry of pre-IPO investors (6 / 18 months).
- Metrics that invalidate a thesis: FCF negative again; margin below RHP trough; receivable/inventory days rising; new debt-funded capex before utilisation fills; promoter pledge; auditor change.
- Sector-hot IPO stocks: default hold period is **zero** unless bought after a ≥30% drawdown from issue with intact fundamentals.
